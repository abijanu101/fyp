**Where things stand.** The whole pipeline runs end to end on full-length classroom recordings:
detection, tracking, per-student features, activity gate, per-labeler models, the retention curve.
Detector and tracker settings are tuned on the full dataset (clean data), and the augmented
versions of those scans are running now. Once the tracker is fixed, the full extraction (52
session-cameras) and the first full-scale training follow. All evaluation so far is on six sessions
with one session per activity, which cannot judge model quality (every model is at chance there,
for structural reasons explained below).

## FYP-1: basic infrastructure and cheap features

### Dataset-specific quirks — done

DIPSER was built around per-student desk cameras; this project uses the classroom (context)
cameras, which the dataset does not annotate. Work done to make it usable
(`docs/experiments/01_dipser.md`):

- **Manual annotation of the context cameras**: a box per student per camera for every in-scope
  session, so tracks can be linked to students' labels (SID–TID association: per-frame Hungarian
  matching with an overlap floor and a guard against people passing through).
- **Quirks found and handled**: the cameras record at about 5 fps, not the 9 fps stated, with 9–45
  dropouts of up to 6 s per session; one session has 314 empty frames (28%); camera numbers 02 and
  03 swap physical positions between days (a per-day camera map, `cameras.yml`); one self-rating of
  7 on a 1–5 scale; annotated students without label files; one group with a single front camera.
- **Download validation**: a remote validator compares the local copy with the FTP server by byte
  ranges, without downloading the 600 GB again.
- **How the labels were made**: the event timestamps show the ratings were entered offline, frame by
  frame at 1 fps (88% of entries fall in the first tenth of a second), as change points that hold
  until the next entry. This decides how labels are interpolated and evaluated
  (`docs/guide/13_from_ratings_to_numbers.md`, O25).

### Well-motivated cheap baseline — done, full-scale run pending

Every component is classical and cheap; the costliest stages run at roughly the camera's frame rate.

- **Detection** (YOLO11n): confidence threshold set by experiment so that no student is lost on the
  full dataset (D1 grid, D2 binary search: 0.22 drops no student; optimum 0.166). The augmented
  version (D3: every frame under 5 random camera degradations) is running.
- **Tracking**: BoT-SORT and StrongSORT tuned on all 52 sessions against the manual boxes (T1):
  BoT-SORT 0.905 IDF1 at about 2 ms per frame, StrongSORT 0.878 with frequent identity switches at
  60× the cost. Augmented scans (T2) are next.
- **Per-student features** (behaviour feature mining units): Kalman/box-geometry motion, residual
  optical flow (motion of the body relative to the box), and soft student-behaviour detection scores
  (SCB: hand raising, reading, writing).
- **Infrastructure**: a two-pass design (expensive frame pass cached once; feature pass in about a
  second per session), every component chosen by name in one configuration file, leave-one-group-out
  evaluation, data augmentation on the fly (colour, noise, flips, occluders, seeded per session),
  viewers for labels, behaviour detections, augmentation and pipeline output, and 93 unit tests.
- **Measured cost**: about 185 ms per frame end to end on full sessions (a 45-minute session in about
  40 minutes); residual flow and SCB dominate.
- **Baselines that define "chance"**: on DIPSER's labels, always predicting a rating of 3 is within
  one point of the majority vote 99% of the time and beats several published results on their own
  metrics (`docs/misc/benchmarks.md`). Every result is reported against such constants.

### Dynamic-clustering statistical features — deferred (not in this deliverable)

The cluster feature mining unit has its slot in the pipeline (a registered "null" extractor that
any clustering extractor replaces without other changes) and its statistical core is implemented
and tested: the spread decomposition behind the two planned features,

- **intra-cluster variance** (*chaos*: how differently students within a group behave), and
- **inter-cluster variance** (*class fragmentation*: how far groups of students diverge),

with the identity within² + between² = total² checked per feature (`src/cfmu/spread.py`). The
clustering itself is for FYP-2 (soft dynamic clustering).

### Instructional-activity mixture of experts — built, awaiting the full dataset

A gate predicts the instructional activity (six DIPSER activities, from scene features) and weights
activity-specific engagement experts; per-experiment or shared experts, soft or hard mixing, and an
oracle condition (the true activity) to separate gate errors from expert quality. All of it is
classical (random forests, logistic regression) and runs in milliseconds.

The alternative in the literature is a vision-language model: the dataset authors prompt
LLaVA-34B with images of the student. That is expensive per frame and, across students, scored
0.116 weighted accuracy, below a constant prediction (`docs/misc/benchmarks.md` §3.1).

**Evaluation is planned as the full pipeline experiment** (`docs/experiments/06_full_pipeline.md`):
two otherwise identical full-scale runs, the mixture against a single decoder, compared entry for
entry, overall and per activity. On the six-session run each activity appears in one session only, so
holding out a group holds out its activities entirely: the gate scores 0% and activity experts have
no training data by construction (`docs/experiments/04_six_session_run.md` §4). The full extraction
gives each activity three groups and three cameras.

### Labeler agreement problem — analysed, training redesigned around it

- **The problem.** Each student is rated by four experts and themselves. Agreement is near chance
  (Krippendorff's ordinal α between −0.08 and 0.13 per activity); labelers use the scale very
  differently (one sits at 3 for 88% of the time, another at 2 for 73%); one expert made two thirds
  of the expert entries on the six sessions; half of all student-window consensus values sit exactly
  on the rating 3.
- **What "the label" means.** A taxonomy of possible targets (unit × aggregation × labelers: 36
  distinct targets), the constant baseline of each, and where each published work sits
  (`docs/misc/targets.md`).
- **The design that follows** (implemented, the default; guide Chapter 7 §7.9): no averaging of
  labelers in training. One model per labeler (four experts and the self-report), each trained on
  windows centred on that labeler's own rating entries, with the rating as a soft target and a
  configurable interpolation of the labels. Evaluation is per label: every rating as it was entered,
  experts and self as separate tracks. The retention curve shows each labeler model's curve and
  their median (or their majority vote), so disagreement stays visible instead of being averaged
  away.
- **First run.** On the six sessions everything works end to end and every prediction is at chance,
  for the structural reason above (`docs/experiments/05_per_labeler.md`). The features themselves
  do carry consistent within-session signal: class motion falls when more students are rated
  attentive (Spearman ρ ≈ −0.3; `04_six_session_run.md` §6).

## FYP-2: more sophisticated features

### Deep-learning approaches — planned

A learned merging function over each student's features, and a transformer-based decoder that
takes [v_student, v_cluster] (the student's own features with their cluster's), replacing the
classical experts. The per-labeler targets, per-label evaluation and trivial baselines of FYP-1
carry over unchanged, so learned models are compared against the same constants and the same
classical baseline.

### Soft dynamic clustering — planned

Clustering students by behaviour within each window, with soft memberships, to compute the
intra- and inter-cluster variance features (chaos, fragmentation) whose maths is already in place.

### Instructional-activity mixture of experts II — planned

The FYP-1 mixture with learned components: a gate and experts that use the learned features above.
