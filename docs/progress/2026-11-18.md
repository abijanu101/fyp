## What changed

**The question changed from "how to transform the features" to "what information is missing".**
Experiments 07 and 08 tried every transformation within reach: tuning k-NN's k from 1 to 1,000,
PCA from 5 components to all of them, and dropping the 68 class-context columns, on logistic
regression, a random forest and k-NN. The best held-out ranking of the experts' labels moved within
Spearman 0.08–0.11, against 0.14 for the other experts' mean, and the self-report stayed at chance in
every arm. The study of the 95 inputs (08 §1, Appendix A) found one per-student signal, optical flow
inside the box, at ρ ≈ 0.08. The models are limited by the signal in the input.

**The candidate for more information is visual focus of attention**: where each student's head is
pointed. The classroom literature measures attention mostly through head pose and gaze (Goldberg et
al. 2019), its synchrony findings are about head rotation (Raca et al.), and the one outside DIPSER
work found head pose alone carried most of the signal (`docs/misc/benchmarks.md` §3.4). None of our
95 inputs measures the head.

**Decisions.**
- Head direction is estimated from the classroom cameras (not DIPSER's per-student desk cameras),
  keeping the setting of FYP-1, and used as new inputs to the rating models, scored like 06–08.
- A keypoint pose model on the tracked students gives yaw and pitch proxies and face-visibility
  flags. Heads are about 35–50 px wide at full resolution, so the pilot compares the pose model's
  input resolution before committing.
- The frames are re-extracted from scratch with the new unit and 4 augmented copies (head pose is
  the kind of input that overfits to camera angle and seat, which the copies vary).
- Training targets are the entered rating (no interpolation); metrics are reported train / test with
  a prediction profile and human-ceiling reference rows; the C-index is computed but introduced in a
  later experiment.
